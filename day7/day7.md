# Choosing the Right Claude Model & Reasoning Level

Just like eating sushi with a fork or pizza with chopsticks, every task needs its own tool. Claude offers different models and reasoning effort levels optimized for specific workloads. Selecting the right combination improves output quality and maximizes cost efficiency.

## 🤖 The Model Lineup

* **Claude 3.5 Haiku**: Blazing fast and highly cost-effective. Best for quick data lookups, text classification, and high-volume chatbots.
* **Claude 3.5 Sonnet**: The ideal balance of speed and intelligence. Perfect for general writing, everyday coding, and deep content creation.
* **Claude 3 Opus**: Maximum power for deep analysis. Designed for complex scientific data, long-form research, and high-level strategy.

## 🧠 Reasoning Effort Levels

Adjusting the reasoning effort controls how long Claude analyzes a problem before responding:

* **Low Effort**: Instant replies. Use this when speed is more important than deep analysis.
* **Medium Effort**: Balanced speed and accuracy. Ideal for standard, multi-step tasks.
* **High Effort**: Deep, slow thinking. Best for advanced math, tough logic puzzles, and debugging complex code.

# My Claude Usage Strategy

## Recommended Primary Model
**Claude Sonnet** (currently Sonnet 5) — this is Anthropic's default, everyday-workhorse tier, and it's the right home base for almost everything on your list.

## Why This Model Fits Me
You're not doing high-volume, trivial lookups (which would favor Haiku), and you're not doing research-lab-grade, multi-hour agentic work (which would justify Opus's cost every time). You're a student juggling coding practice, concept learning, and career-document work — Sonnet gives you strong reasoning, solid coding ability, and a large context window, at a price/speed point that holds up to daily use without burning through your usage limits.

## When to Use Haiku
Haiku is Anthropic's fastest, cheapest tier — great for volume, not depth.
- Quick syntax lookups ("what's the Python list comprehension syntax for...")
- Rewording or reformatting something you already wrote
- Rapid-fire Q&A while studying flashcard-style
- Simple debugging of an obvious one-line error

## When to Use Sonnet
Your default 80% model.
- Writing and debugging code for class projects or personal builds (like your Flask apps)
- Explaining concepts in depth — DSA, OS, networking, AI/ML fundamentals
- Drafting and iterating on resumes, cover letters, LinkedIn content
- Planning study schedules or breaking down a new topic into a roadmap

## When to Use Opus
Reach for Opus when the task is genuinely hard, ambiguous, or long-horizon — not for routine work.
- Deep research questions requiring careful synthesis across many sources or a long document
- Architecting a multi-file coding project from scratch, or debugging a gnarly bug that's resisted Sonnet
- High-stakes writing — a final internship application, a technical interview take-home, a capstone project writeup
- Any time Sonnet's answer feels shallow or you're going back and forth without progress

## Recommended Effort Level

### Low
Fast factual lookups, simple rewrites, quick syntax checks — anything where you'd rather have speed than depth.

### Standard (default)
Your everyday setting. Coding help, concept explanations, resume edits, day-to-day learning support. Leave it here unless you have a reason to change it.

### High
Deep research questions, multi-step coding problems, anything where you want Claude to reason carefully before answering rather than give you the first plausible response.

### Max
Reserve for your hardest, highest-stakes moments — a genuinely difficult research synthesis, a complex debugging session that's stumped you, or a critical document (like a final resume pass before submitting an internship application) where you want maximum scrutiny.

## Biggest Mistakes I Should Avoid
- **Defaulting to the most powerful model out of habit.** Opus on a syntax question wastes time and usage limit for zero quality gain.
- **Using Max effort as your default.** It slows down responses and eats your daily limit fast — save it for when you're actually stuck.
- **Under-using Opus when you should upgrade.** If Sonnet's answer to a hard research or debugging question feels thin or you're going in circles, that's your signal to switch up, not to keep re-prompting Sonnet.
- **Treating effort and model choice as separate levers you rarely touch.** Tuning effort within Sonnet is often smarter than jumping straight to Opus.
- **Not distinguishing "learning" from "producing."** When you're learning a concept, Standard effort with follow-up questions beats one giant Max-effort mega-answer — you retain more by going back and forth.

## Final Recommendation
If you could only pick **one model and one effort level** for most of your work: **Claude Sonnet at Standard effort.**

It covers coding, learning, and resume/career work well, keeps you comfortably inside your daily usage limits as a heavy daily user, and stays fast enough that you won't get frustrated waiting on routine questions. Bump to Opus + High only when you hit something genuinely hard — that's the 20% of cases where the upgrade actually pays off.
